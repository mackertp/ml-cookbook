How Sequencing Works

The animation below explains the process of how a sample is sequenced. Sequencing is used for a variety of reasons: picking targeted cancer therapies, diagnosing rare Mendelian diseases, adjusting an individual's drug dose, matching a transplant, or tracking a resistant pathogen.

End-to-end clinical testing can run from a few hundred dollars for a focused gene panel to roughly $1,000–$5,000+ for clinical exome or genome work. Sequencing is not typically used when imaging and pathology have already given a clear treatment path or if phenotype-directed treatments are better suited to address the indication.
